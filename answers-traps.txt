RISC-V assembly answers, verified from run43582093340 user/call.asm (GCC riscv64-linux-gnu).
1. Integer/pointer arguments use a0-a7; printf receives its format in a0, computed 12 in a1 and 13 in a2.
2. GCC may inline f and g in main, replacing f(8)+1 with constant 12. The standalone f/g functions may still exist without being called by main. Inspect the actual remote disassembly.
3. printf is at 0x70e in this build. main starts at 0x1c; li a2,13 at 0x24 and li a1,12 at 0x26. main has no f/g call: both were inlined/constant-folded; standalone g and f still exist at 0 and 0xe, and f contains addiw rather than a call to g.
4. The handout says jalr, but this linked build uses a relaxed jal at 0x30 targeting printf (0x70e). ra is the next instruction, 0x34. A non-relaxed jalr would likewise save its own PC+4, not necessarily these addresses.
5. Little endian i bytes 72 6c 64 00 spell rld, 57616 hex is E110 (this printf uses uppercase digits), so output is HE110 World. Big endian use i=0x726c6400, keep 57616 unchanged because integer hex formatting is independent of byte order.
6. printf("x=%d y=%d",3) has no second integer argument; its variadic access has undefined behavior. No specific value after y= is guaranteed (it may observe an unrelated a2 value).
