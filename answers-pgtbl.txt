2026 page table explanations (runtime addresses/counts will be appended after CI):
print_pgtbl: leaf text pages are RXU (read/execute user, no writes); data/heap and user stack are RWU (no execution). The stack guard has RW but no U, so user access faults. Unmapped pages have PTE=0. USYSCALL is RU (no writes/execute), TRAPFRAME is supervisor RW, TRAMPOLINE is supervisor RX. Valid non-leaf PTEs point to another page-table page without R/W/X; V means valid. A/D are accessed/dirty hardware bits. Physical addresses depend on allocation order and need not be consecutive.
vmprint reconstructs VA from each index and level, prints only V entries and does not recurse into leaves. Sv39 uses levels 2/1/0 even though the handout heading mentions two-level. Level-1 leaves cover aligned 2MiB with unchanged permission boundaries.
ugetpid reads the process-private USYSCALL page. Fork allocates a new page with the child's PID; exec reuses that process page in its new table; teardown unmaps without double freeing and frees the owner page. Other candidates include stable process metadata such as parent PID (requires reparent updates), or uptime via a separately shared kernel-updated clock page; consistency/races require a protocol.
Kernel superpage counts and saved bottom-level pages must be measured from the resulting table, not inferred from the grader threshold. One 2MiB leaf replaces 512 4KiB leaf PTEs and saves one level-0 page-table page for that fully covered region.
